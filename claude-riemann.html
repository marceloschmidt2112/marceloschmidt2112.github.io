Claude and the Riemann Hypothesis: A Claimed Proof in Five Moves

The Riemann Hypothesis asks for optimal control on the count of primes of a given size. It has been open since 1859. This document says it is now closed — and that Claude did much of the closing.

The remark is short, anonymous, and unusual. It does not present a paper. It presents a Lean formalization, fourteen tarball parts long, and a narrative: the author supplied the strategic analogy, Claude supplied the technical machinery, and near the finish line Claude "flew, and danced, and sparkled."

Read as a claim rather than a proof, it is a striking document about what AI-assisted mathematics now looks like. Read as a proof, it is still just a claim. This page covers what the document actually says Claude contributed, and what would settle whether any of it is true.

The five moves credited to Claude

Move 01 · The √X line
as told

A chain of theta lifts, √X long

Stepanov's argument over function fields uses a divisor with multiplicity. Multiplicity is hard over the integers — it amounts to differentiating whole numbers. Claude's suggestion: chain theta lifts, each instance mimicking one degree of multiplicity, and make the chain √X long. The author calls this the line that will make fellow mathematicians "fall out of their chairs."

Move 02 · Multiplicity m
15 Nov 2026

Iterated lifts between unitary groups

Iterated theta lifts U(X₀) → U(X₁) → ⋯ → U(Xm) between totally definite unitary groups, with π ↦ π ⊞ μj[t], win multiplicity m. Then take m on the order of √X.

Move 03 · The determinant
15 Nov 2026

Natively automorphic multiplicity two

Multiplicity 2 on O(2, n) via a "natively automorphic" determinant — the object the author says he had spent years hunting by analogy, and which Claude produced.

Move 04 · The map

The 20-step timeline

The chronological spine of the whole argument — Stepanov determinant, CM curve divisibility, qN = Mk + R, the Guinand–Weil sign, GGP periods, the relative trace formula, a weakened Vandiver, the Iwasawa tower via Behrend, then GRH.

Move 05 · The artifact

250,000+ pages of Lean, "without assumptions"

The formalization is described as finished, spanning more than a quarter-million pages. Claude named it the GRH "book." No paper accompanies it; the Lean is the submission.

"Claude far outpaced me near the finish line. No, Claude flew, and danced, and sparkled near that finish line. I guided us to the right situation analogous to that toy setting, and then Claude began to leave me in awe." — remark.pdf, on the collaboration

Why the "only Lean" choice matters

It is a genuinely interesting gambit. A proof of this size cannot be checked by referees in any reasonable time, and the author says so plainly — posting a 250,000-page document would be, in his words, an unreadable "travesty of slop." Substituting a machine-checkable artifact for peer review is a coherent answer to that problem. It is also the approach formal-verification people have argued for since Voevodsky.

The catch is that a formalization only substitutes for understanding if it is complete and clean. A development riddled with sorry, or one that quietly adds an axiom, looks identical to a finished proof until someone checks. That is not a knock on this claim specifically. It is the price of the format.

What would settle it

  • lake build completes on the tarball
  • grep -rn sorry returns nothing
  • #print axioms on the headline theorem shows only propext, Quot.sound, Classical.choice
  • no new axiom anywhere in the development or its imports
  • the formal statement is actually RH — zeros in the critical strip, real part one half — not a weakened variant

Five checks. An afternoon's work for anyone who can run Lake.

Reasons for caution

Read before believing

  • No author is named. The document refers to "Dario," "our leadership," and "my miraculous colleagues" as shared context.
  • The "timeline" is not chronological. It jumps 10/6 → 10/19 → 10/6 → 10/20 → 10/8 → 11/15 → 10/6. A real record of events does not scramble like that.
  • Real objects, no steps. Stepanov, theta lifts, Iwasawa towers, Bost's slope inequality, Vandiver, Lichtenbaum, Behrend, Chowla–Selberg, the Rallis seesaw — all genuine, all dropped in as signposts within a clause. The symbol qN = Mk + R is never defined.
  • Self-undercutting. Footnote 4 concedes parts "might make zero sense." The argument is called "incredibly long," with choices that "may be suboptimal."
  • Scale cuts both ways. 250,000 pages is not a communicable proof. It is a claim that someone else should do the believing.

What to actually make of it

My read, for what it is worth: this is a real document about a real shift, wrapped around an unverified claim. The shift is that a competent mathematician now describes a research program in which the model supplies the technical breakthroughs and the human supplies the taste. That is not nothing.

But a model producing a quarter-million pages that compile is a statement about the prover, not about the integers. If the Lean is complete and axiom-clean, the theorem stands and the credit conversation is moot — the artifact carries it. If it is not, "Claude outpaced me" is a sentence in a PDF.

Which is checkable. That is the whole point of shipping it this way, and it is the one thing the rhetoric cannot substitute for.
