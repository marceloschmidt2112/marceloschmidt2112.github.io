He's on the GNC team

Building the flight brain of a self-landing rocket.
ARCS at ASU Project GRACE GNC subteam
🚀 Translation: GNC = Guidance, Navigation, and Control. It is the single hardest job on a self-landing rocket. Simon is on the team writing the software brain that decides — 200 times per second — how much to throttle the engine and which way to point the nozzle so the rocket doesn't crash.

🧭What GNC actually means, in plain English

G
Guidance — "Where do I want to go, and what path gets me there?" Plans the trajectory: launch profile, coast, descent, landing burn.
N
Navigation — "Where am I right now, how fast, and which way am I pointing?" Fuses GPS + gyros + accelerometers + barometer, 200+ times per second.
C
Control — "What do I do about it?" Adjusts engine throttle and thrust vector so reality matches the plan. Milliseconds of latency matter.

This is the same problem SpaceX had to solve for Falcon 9. It sits at the intersection of control theory, real-time software, sensor fusion, and flight dynamics. Graduate-level material — and he's doing it as an undergrad.

Aaryan Anand

🛠️What his day-to-day likely involves

  • Control-loop tuning — writing/modifying PID or MPC (Model Predictive Control) code that keeps the rocket vertical
  • Kalman filter work — the math that fuses noisy sensors into a clean estimate of position/velocity/attitude
  • Simulation — running SITL (Software-In-The-Loop) tests before every flight; they have a SITL CSV pipeline visible in the repo
  • Trajectory design — computing optimal ascent/descent profiles
  • Hardware-in-the-loop testing — running the flight computer with fake sensor inputs
  • Post-flight data analysis — pulling logs from flights, figuring out what worked and what didn't
  • C/C++, Python, or Rust on embedded hardware — likely Teensy or NXP microcontrollers (they have a TeensyXpresso repo)

🎯Why GNC is the crown-jewel subteam

Every subteam on a rocket matters, but GNC is the one where if you get it wrong, nothing else matters. The engine can be perfect, the composites beautiful, the electronics flawless — if GNC can't keep the rocket pointed and control the descent, you get a crater. That's why GNC leads are often the technical face of any rocket program.

On resume terms: "GNC engineer, propulsive-lander project" is arguably the single highest-signal line an aerospace undergrad can put on paper.
