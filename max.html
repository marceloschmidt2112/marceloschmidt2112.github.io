The cool part · Max's actual project

🔬 Adaptive Patterning® — lithography that absorbs manufacturing errors

Traditional semiconductor lithography assumes every die lands in a perfect grid, in a perfect position, at a perfect angle. Reality never cooperates: dies drift, rotate, and warp during packaging. Traditional processes throw those parts out. That's the yield problem that eats a huge chunk of manufacturing cost.

Adaptive Patterning flips the model. Instead of forcing the pattern onto the die, it measures where every die actually is — then dynamically generates a custom lithography pattern that lines up with reality. The pattern adapts to the parts, not the other way around.

Result: variances and misalignments that would have been defects become… nothing. The pattern just absorbs them. Yield jumps. Cost drops. Chips get built that otherwise couldn't be.

This is not a small-detail improvement. Adaptive Patterning is one of the reasons Deca can ship packaging at the volume they do — over 6 billion devices in the field use it. It's a genuine "the industry needed this and they solved it" innovation. And Max is on the team.

▸ Research

🛰️Radiation Hardening · SCALE program at ASU

Full-time undergraduate research assistant · Apr 2026 – present
Radiation-Hardened Microelectronics

Max is part of SCALE — Scalable Asymmetric Lifecycle Engagement — the DoD-funded microelectronics workforce program (Purdue-led, national scope). SCALE trains US students in the exact skills the Department of Defense needs to secure its chip supply chain.

His subfield: Radiation Hardening. This is designing chips that keep working in space, satellites, missiles, and nuclear environments — where cosmic rays and gamma bursts would fry a normal consumer IC. It's a small, specialized field with a very tight talent pool.

Why this matters: rad-hard microelectronics engineers are a national-security specialty. The talent pipeline is deliberately small, salaries are strong, and many of the roles are cleared. Getting into SCALE as an undergrad is a signal — the department picked him for a program the DoD is funding to solve a real problem.
